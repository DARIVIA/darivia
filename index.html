Decision Trajectory Intelligence (DTI)

Modeling how consumers evaluate, reject, and choose products.

Reveals what happens between product fit and final choice — and turns consumer decision processes into structured intelligence.

PROBLEM
The outcome doesn't explain the decision.
Brands see outcomes — sales, conversion, returns, markdowns.
But they rarely see the decision process that produced them:
·  Why a product entered consideration.
·  Why it was rejected.
·  What displaced it.
·  Where product-consumer mismatches emerged.
·  How changing goals, preferences, and context altered the choice.
GAP
Product fit is not consumer choice.
A product can fit a customer's needs and still never enter serious consideration.

Consumer choice emerges from the interaction between:

Customer
identity · preferences · parameters

Mission
goal · context · constraints

Product
attributes · positioning · alternatives

Traditional customer and product data capture parts of this system.
They rarely capture the trajectory of the decision itself.
MARKET SHIFT
AI makes decision trajectories observable.

Product discovery and selection are increasingly mediated by AI.

For the first time, interactions between consumers, goals, context, products, and alternatives can be observed and structured at scale.
This creates a new intelligence layer between traditional customer data and commercial outcomes:

Decision Trajectory Intelligence.
WHAT WE DO
DTI models consumer decision processes as decision trajectories and turns them into structured intelligence.

This intelligence can be used to:
·  Understand why products win, lose, or never enter consideration.
·  Identify consumer-assortment gaps and product overlap.
·  Detect substitution and competitive displacement.
·  Model how changing consumer priorities reshape product choice.
·  Provide AI agents with domain-specific decision expertise.
HOW
DTI models the interaction between:

Consumer Mind Model
Who is making the decision.

Goal & Context
What the consumer is trying to achieve and under what conditions.

Product Universe
What products are available and how they differ.

Decision Process
What enters consideration, what is rejected, what survives, and what displaces what.

Outcome
What is ultimately chosen - and why.

Consumer → Mission → Consideration → Comparison → Displacement → Choice
